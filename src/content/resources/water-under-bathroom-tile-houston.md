Your bathroom tile may look perfectly fine on the surface while moisture does damage underneath. For Houston homeowners, water under bathroom tile is especially common because high humidity, heavy rainfall, plumbing issues and the constant water exposure of a bathroom all work on the same few square feet.

Water under tile does not always create an obvious leak. By the time you notice a loose tile, a bad smell or soft flooring, moisture may already be affecting the materials below.

## Signs there is water under your bathroom tile

### 1. Tiles feel loose or move when you step on them

Movement is one of the clearest signs. Tile is set in mortar or adhesive that bonds it to the surface underneath, and water that gets below the tile can weaken that bond over time. Look for:

- A tile that shifts slightly when stepped on
- Cracked tiles near the shower or bathtub
- Uneven areas on the floor
- Loose grout around certain tiles

In Houston homes this often traces back to plumbing leaks, failing shower seals, cracked grout or water getting in around fixtures. A healthy tile floor should feel solid and stable.

### 2. The tile sounds hollow when you tap it

Gently tap different areas of the floor with a coin or the handle of a screwdriver. A well-bonded tile sounds solid. A hollow or echoing sound can mean the tile has lost contact with the surface underneath, which can happen when moisture damages the mortar or adhesive.

A hollow sound does not always mean water is present, but it does mean the area deserves a closer look.

### 3. Grout that stays dark or never seems to dry

Grout is often the first place a moisture problem shows. Watch for:

- Grout lines that stay dark or wet-looking
- Yellow, brown or black discoloration
- Crumbling grout
- Cracks around the shower or bathtub

Water can pass through damaged grout and become trapped beneath the tile. In Houston's humid climate, damp areas take longer to dry, which raises the chance of mold growth.

### 4. A musty smell that will not go away

A damp, earthy or musty odor that returns after cleaning can point to hidden water damage, moisture buildup, mold growth or a slow plumbing leak. Many homeowners notice the smell before they see any damage.

### 5. White powder along the grout lines

A white, chalky residue on grout or tile is often efflorescence. It forms when water moves through tile or grout and leaves minerals behind as it evaporates. It does not always mean severe damage, but it does show that moisture is moving through the floor assembly.

### 6. Soft or spongy areas underfoot

A bathroom floor should feel firm. Areas that feel soft, spongy, bouncy or uneven can mean water has reached the subfloor. Trapped moisture weakens wood materials, causes swelling and turns a small repair into a larger one.

## How to check for moisture under bathroom tile

### Use a moisture meter

A moisture meter can flag areas with abnormal readings without removing tile. Contractors use moisture detection tools to find the problem area and judge whether the damage is isolated or spreading.

### Inspect the places bathrooms usually leak

Most bathroom moisture problems start where water is constant. Check:

- Shower corners
- Bathtub edges
- The toilet base
- Sink plumbing connections
- Cracked grout
- Damaged or missing caulk

A small gap in caulk or grout can let water travel slowly under the tile.

### Check the ceiling below an upstairs bathroom

If the bathroom is on a second floor, look at the ceiling underneath for brown water stains, peeling paint, bubbling drywall or soft spots. ## Will water dry out under bathroom tile?

Small amounts of moisture sometimes evaporate on their own, but water trapped under tile has very little airflow. If the source is not fixed, the problem keeps coming back. Drying the top of the tile does not remove water underneath the floor.

## How do you know if there is mold under the tile?

Mold under tile is often hidden. Possible warning signs include a persistent musty odor, dark staining around grout, repeated moisture problems and past water damage that was never repaired. Houston's warm, humid air keeps damp areas wet longer, which makes those conditions easier for mold.

## How Houston homeowners can prevent water damage under tile

The least expensive repair is the one caught early:

- Repair cracked grout quickly
- Replace failing caulk around showers and tubs
- Fix plumbing leaks right away
- Run the bathroom exhaust fan during and after showers
- Pay attention to any change in tile movement or smell

Bathrooms are built to handle water, but no seal or material lasts forever.

## When should you call a professional?

If you notice loose tiles, a musty smell, soft flooring or other signs of water damage, have the area inspected. Hidden moisture can damage tile, subfloors, drywall and framing if it is ignored.
