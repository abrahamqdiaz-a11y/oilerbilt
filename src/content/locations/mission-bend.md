## Existing homes reveal conditions gradually

Removal is often when the real condition becomes visible. Cabinets can hide old sink leaks. Shower tile can hide soft backing. A ceiling patch can reveal that the plumbing access hole was never supported correctly. We plan from what can be inspected, then document concealed conditions if they appear. A homeowner should know why the scope changed before added work moves forward.

Slab movement is another southwest-side reality. Drywall cracks may be cosmetic, but repeated diagonal cracking, uneven floors and sticking openings deserve more than another skim coat. OilerBilt does not diagnose foundations; the goal is to avoid selling finish work as a cure for building movement.

## Weather-aware repairs

Mission Bend homes face Gulf Coast humidity, heavy rain and occasional freeze events. Interior rebuilds after a burst pipe may involve more than drywall: the cavity needs to dry, damaged insulation or trim may need attention, and finish coats must blend with aged paint. Bathroom materials should work together as a moisture system rather than rely on one “water-resistant” label.

Exterior stone paths and borders also need grade and drainage review. A pretty path that sends rain toward the slab or creates a low spot is not an upgrade. Downspouts, gates and existing routes are part of layout.
