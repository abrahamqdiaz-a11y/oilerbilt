## A walkway has to do more than look finished

A stone path should give people a stable route, meet doors and drives cleanly, and move water in a sensible direction. OilerBilt installs stone walkways, garden borders and focused exterior upgrades around Houston homes. The work begins with grade, access and how the area behaves after a hard rain.

Houston soil movement can shift poorly prepared exterior work. A decorative stone placed on soft or uneven ground may rock, settle or hold water. Base preparation and edge restraint depend on the material and intended use. A garden border has different demands than the route guests use every day.

## Layout and transitions

Curves should feel deliberate and remain wide enough to walk. Steps, gates, downspouts, irrigation and utility routes can control the design. We look at how the path meets the driveway, porch or patio because awkward transitions are where trip points and puddles tend to appear.

Material selection affects more than color. Thickness, texture, size variation and availability all influence the installation. Samples should be reviewed in outdoor light, and natural variation should be expected with stone.

## Rain, drainage and Houston sites

Heavy Gulf Coast rain makes drainage part of even a small exterior project. New work should not intentionally send runoff toward the foundation or block an existing route. Downspouts, low areas and neighboring grades need to be visible before final layout.

Some properties are inside municipalities; others are in unincorporated Harris County. Utility locations, drainage easements and HOA rules may apply even when a building permit does not. The project address and exact work determine what needs checking.
