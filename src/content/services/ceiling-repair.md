## A damaged ceiling is a symptom, not the problem

Ceilings fail differently than walls. Gravity works against the repair, side lighting from windows and fixtures exposes every imperfection, and the damage is almost always caused by something in the space above. A stain, a sag or a seam that keeps reopening is the visible end of a roof leak, a supply line, an air-conditioning drain pan, a second-floor bathroom or normal structural movement.

OilerBilt repairs ceilings across Houston and Harris County. That covers water-stained and leak-damaged ceilings, sagging or separated board, cracks and failed tape joints, access holes left by other trades, texture matching, popcorn removal and paint-ready finishing. The first step is understanding what caused the failure, because a ceiling closed over an active leak fails a second time.

### Water-stained and leak-damaged ceilings

A brown ring means water arrived, spread across the board and dried at the edges. Drywall that has absorbed water loses strength, and the paper face can separate from the gypsum core even when the surface still looks flat. Insulation above the ceiling holds moisture longer than the board does.

The water source has to be stopped first, by a roofer, plumber or HVAC contractor as appropriate. Then the affected area can be opened, wet material removed and the cavity given time to dry before new board goes up. Stain-blocking primer has a place in the finish, but it is not a substitute for removing material that is still damp.

### Sagging and separated ceilings

Ceiling drywall sags when it has taken on water, when fasteners have pulled through, when the original board was hung too thin for the joist spacing, or when blown-in insulation was added above an already marginal installation. Sagging is not cosmetic. A ceiling that feels soft underfoot from the attic side, or that shows daylight-width gaps at the seams, should be evaluated before anything is fastened back to it.

### Cracks and failed tape joints

Straight cracks that follow a seam usually mean the tape joint failed, from movement, from an under-filled original installation or from years of expansion and contraction in an unconditioned attic above. Cracks that run diagonally from a corner, especially paired with sticking doors or cracked exterior masonry, can point to slab movement. A ceiling patch restores the surface; it does not stabilize a foundation, and we say so rather than selling a repair that will reopen.

### Texture matching on ceilings

Ceiling texture is the hardest part of the job to make invisible. Spray orange peel, knockdown, hand-applied patterns and older stomped or swirled textures all behave differently, and decades of paint buildup change how a new application sits next to an old one. Raking light across a ceiling from a window or a can light will find a blend that a wall would have hidden. We look at the lighting and the texture before the work starts and give an honest answer about how wide the blend needs to be.

### Popcorn ceiling removal

Popcorn texture is common in Houston-area houses built from the 1960s through the 1980s. Removal is more than scraping: painted texture resists release, old patches and seam work show once the surface is bare, and the ceiling normally needs skim coating, sanding, primer and paint to finish smooth. Unknown older materials should be handled honestly, and testing may be appropriate before the surface is disturbed.

### Plaster ceilings in older homes

Houston neighborhoods developed before the Second World War, including much of River Oaks and the older inner-loop streets, often have plaster over wood lath rather than gypsum board. Plaster cracks, keys break loose from the lath, and patching it with a drywall approach can leave a hard line at the transition. Decorative plaster, cove detail and crown molding raise the difficulty further. These ceilings are reviewed in person because the right repair depends on how the original assembly is holding up.

### High and vaulted ceilings

Two-story entries, vaulted family rooms and older homes with tall ceilings add access, staging and safety to the scope before any finishing happens. Furniture protection, containment and dust control also matter more when the work is overhead. These factors show up in the estimate rather than as a surprise on the day of the work.

### Permits and jurisdiction

Finish repair on a ceiling usually differs from work that changes plumbing, electrical systems or structural framing, and requirements differ between the City of Houston and unincorporated Harris County. We confirm the project address and the actual scope before saying whether a permit is involved. Broad internet answers are not a substitute for the authority with jurisdiction.
